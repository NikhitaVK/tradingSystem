CRITERION 1 — ECONOMIC MECHANISM
Is the stated mechanism a real, durable market inefficiency, or is it post-hoc rationalisation?
Ask yourself: "If I showed this strategy spec to someone without the backtest results, would they believe the mechanism makes sense from first principles?"
Red flags: mechanism is vague, relies on "patterns" without a behavioural explanation, or only works if you squint at the data.

CRITERION 2 — OVERFITTING CHECK
Walk-Forward Efficiency (WFE) = out-of-sample Sharpe / in-sample Sharpe.
WFE = {wfe}
Threshold: WFE >= 0.5 required.
If WFE < 0.5, the strategy is fitting in-sample noise, not a real signal. Challenge this even if Sharpe looks good — an overfit strategy will not replicate.
If WFE is None (insufficient slices), flag as inconclusive and note it as a risk.

CRITERION 3 — REGIME ROBUSTNESS
Regime breakdown: {regime_breakdown}
Does this strategy produce trades across multiple market regimes (bull, bear, sideways)?
A strategy that only fires in one regime is not robust — it will sit idle or lose badly when the regime changes.
Challenge: "What happens to this strategy when the regime shifts?"

CRITERION 4 — COST SURVIVAL
Profit factor = {profit_factor}
Threshold: >= 1.5 required (gross profit must exceed gross loss by 50% margin to survive slippage, spreads, and unexpected costs).
A profit factor of 1.0–1.5 looks profitable in backtest but is fragile in live trading. Challenge the cost model assumptions.
